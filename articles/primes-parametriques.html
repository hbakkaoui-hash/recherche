Une famille paramétrique de nombres premiers $p=k\,m(m+1)+e+2kq$

Hassane Bakkaoui · Chercheur indépendant

PDF arXiv

Epistemic key (read first). Four levels of certainty are tagged throughout:

\(\blacksquare\) RIGOROUS: unconditional, proved from classical theorems.
\(\blacksquare\) CONDITIONAL: established under explicit hypotheses (GRH, RH, Bateman–Horn).
\(\blacksquare\) HEURISTIC: predicted under standard heuristics and validated numerically.
\(\blacksquare\) NEGATIVE: rigorous invalidation of a previously reported claim (including the author’s own).

No result is presented as an unconditional breakthrough.

Introduction

Motivation and programme

This paper undertakes a theoretical exploration of the distribution of primes through a parametric approach. We rewrite primes under a doubly indexed parametric form in order to analyse structures, regularities, and concentration phenomena observable within specific parametric frames—not in the sense of a strict periodicity, but as the possible existence of arithmetic constraints. We concentrate throughout on what can be demonstrated and on observations formulable within a rigorous framework. The paper does not claim to settle any classical question; it opens a complementary direction of study.

The seed is elementary: every prime \(p>3\) satisfies \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod6\), i.e. \(p\) lies at signed distance one from the nearest multiple of \(6\). The family studied here, \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:family} p_{k,m,\varepsilon,q}=km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq, \end{equation}\] generalises this by replacing \(6\) with \(2k\), the linear variable by the pronic number \(m(m+1)\), and by making the search offset \(q\) explicit. For fixed \(m,\varepsilon\), [eq:family] is an arithmetic progression of common difference \(2k\), to which Dirichlet’s theorem applies unconditionally. The minimal offset \(q_{\min}(k,m,\varepsilon)\)—the integer of least absolute value making \(p\) prime—is the central object.

Three layers describe \(q_{\min}\): the marginal layer (size \(|q_{\min}|\) versus \(m\), §5), the conditional layer (law of \(|q|\) given \(m\), §§4–5), and the modular layer (\(q_{\min}\bmod\ell\), §§3–4), complemented by a fourth computational layer (§§3,7).

The parametric family

We fix \(k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\) admissible, meaning \(\gcd(\varepsilon,2k)=1\) for at least one \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\); set \(E_k:=\{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}:\gcd(\varepsilon,2k)=1\}\). Since \(\gcd(\pm1,n)=1\), \(E_k=\{-1,+1\}\) for all \(k\ge1\). The generalised hexagonal base is \(H^{(k)}_m:=km(m+1)+1\). For \(k=3\), \(\{H^{(3)}_m\}=\{7,19,37,61,91,127,\dots\}\) are the centred hexagonal numbers (OEIS A003215), lattice points of \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\), \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\).

Theorem 1 (Rigorous; degenerate modular axis, Theorem 17). Let \(k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\) and \(\ell\) a prime dividing \(2k\). Then for every admissible \(\varepsilon\), every \(m,q\), \[p_{k,m,\varepsilon,q}\equiv\varepsilon\pmod\ell,\] independently of \(q\). The degenerate modular axis is fixed exactly.

Theorem 2 (Rigorous; Theorem 22). For every \((a,b)\in(\mathbb{N}^{\ast})^2\) such that \(p:=3m(m+1)+1\) with \(m=2^a3^b-1\) is prime, the Pocklington–Lehmer criterion gives an unconditional certificate with \(F=2^a3^{b+1}>\sqrt p\). The algorithm produced a prime \(p^{\ast}=3m^{\ast}(m^{\ast}+1)+1\), \(m^{\ast}=2^{19435}\cdot3^{19173}-1\), of \(29\,998\) decimal digits, certified unconditionally in \(3\)h\(06\)min on consumer hardware.

Theorem 3 (Rigorous, unconditional; Theorem 48). For every fixed \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\), the regression amplitude \(A_N(\gamma)=\tfrac{2}{\pi(X)}\big|\sum_{p\le X}(\{\sqrt{p/3}\}-\tfrac12)\,p^{-i\gamma}\big|\) satisfies \(A_N(\gamma)\to0\) unconditionally (with an explicit power saving \(X^{-\delta}\) under a standard Type II estimate). No ordinate of \(\zeta\) carries a surviving linear signal.

Theorem 4 (Conditional under GRH; Theorem 34). Under GRH, for each fixed admissible \(k,\varepsilon\), \(|q_{\min}(k,m,\varepsilon)|=O_k(m(\log m)^2)\).

Theorem 5 (Conditional under H1+GRH+H3; Theorem 36). For \(k=3\), \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), \(\mathbb{E}[|q|\mid m]=\tfrac{m}{4}+O((\log m)^2/\sqrt m)\).

Theorem 6 (Conditional under RH; Theorem 50). Under RH, the per-prime \(\zeta\)-footprint on the layer occupancy is \(\ll p^{-1/4}(\log p)^2\) (Selberg variance).

 Heuristic laws. \(\mathbb{E}[|q_{\min}|]\sim\log m/C_k\) (\(R^2=0.984\), \(N=10^6\)); \(C_0(k)=1/(4\sqrt k)\) stable to \({<}0.02\%\) across \(3\le k\le29\); and \(q_{\min}\bmod\ell\) is empirically equidistributed for \(\ell\mid2k\).

 Negative result (Prop. 46). At \(N=10^8\): \(R^2=1.16\times10^{-7}\), \(z=-2.20\sigma\); three permutation tests confirm the absence of any spectral signal of \(\zeta(s)\) in \(Q(r)\).

What is and is not claimed

The paper (i) proves a rigorous structural law (the exact degenerate axis); (ii) establishes two genuinely unconditional pillars—the constructive Pocklington record (Theorem 22) and the analytic-negative vanishing of the \(\zeta\)-signal (Theorem 48); (iii) establishes three conditional theorems (C, D, E) under GRH/Bateman–Horn and one conditional \(\zeta\)-footprint bound (Theorem 50) under RH; (iv) validates three heuristic laws at \(N\in\{10^6,10^7,10^8\}\). It claims no unconditional progress on classical problems (parity barrier, uniform PNT at \(N^{1/2}\), RH), and \(C_0(k)=1/(4\sqrt k)\) is a geometric identity, not a mysterious invariant.

A correction of record.

An earlier version stated, as “Theorem A”, that \(q_{\min}\) itself is uniformly distributed modulo \(\ell\) for \(\ell\mid2k\), by reduction to Bombieri–Vinogradov. That reduction is invalid: since all family primes lie in the single class \(\varepsilon\bmod\ell\), the distribution of \(q_{\min}\bmod\ell\) is governed by the position of the first prime in each progression, not by Bombieri–Vinogradov. The empirical equidistribution survives only as a heuristic (Observation 18). Detecting and reclassifying this is part of the discipline of the epistemic key.

The parametric family: structure and elementary properties

Definition 7 (Canonical triple). Given a prime \(p>k+1\) and \(k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\), the canonical triple is the unique \((m,\varepsilon,q)\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\times E_k\times\mathbb{Z}\) with \(p=km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq\) and \(|q|\) minimal. Then \(q_{\min}(k,m,\varepsilon):=q\).

Proposition 8 (Rigorous). For \(k=3\), the sign \(\varepsilon\) of the canonical triple of a prime \(p>3\) is determined by \(p\bmod6\): \(p\equiv1\pmod6\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon=+1\), \(p\equiv5\pmod6\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon=-1\).

Proof. For \(k=3\), [eq:family] reads \(p=3m(m+1)+\varepsilon+6q\). As \(m(m+1)\) is even, \(3m(m+1)\equiv0\pmod6\), so \(p\equiv\varepsilon\pmod6\); the classes coprime to \(6\) are \(\{1,5\}\equiv\{+1,-1\}\). ◻

Theorem 9 (Rigorous; infinitude). For every \(k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\), \(\varepsilon\in E_k\), \(m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\), the set \(\{p_{k,m,\varepsilon,q}:q\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cap\mathbb{P}\) is infinite.

Proof. At fixed \((k,m,\varepsilon)\), [eq:family] is an AP in \(q\) of difference \(2k\) and initial term \(km(m+1)+\varepsilon\), with \(\gcd(2k,km(m+1)+\varepsilon)=\gcd(2k,\varepsilon)=1\). Dirichlet’s theorem applies. ◻

Theorem 10 (Rigorous; upper bound). For every prime \(p>k+1\) and admissible \(k\), \(|q_{\min}(k,m,\varepsilon)|\le\tfrac{m+1}{2}\).

Proof. There is a unique \(m\) with \(km(m+1)+\varepsilon-k(m+1)<p\le km(m+1)+\varepsilon+k(m+1)\) (consecutive values of \(km(m+1)\) differ by \(2k(m+1)\), and centred intervals of half-width \(k(m+1)\) tile \(\mathbb{N}\)). Then \(q=(p-km(m+1)-\varepsilon)/(2k)\) is an integer with \(|q|\le(m+1)/2\); minimality gives the claim. ◻

Observation 11 (\(O(1)\) algorithm). Theorem 10 yields \(m=\big\lfloor\tfrac12(\sqrt{4(p-\varepsilon)/k+1}-1)\big\rfloor\) with \(\varepsilon\) fixed by \(p\bmod2k\); inverting [eq:family] gives \(q\). The triple is computed in constant time, without factorisation.

Proposition 12 (Rigorous, limiting form). Fix \(k\), \(\varepsilon\in E_k\), and let \(L^{(k,\varepsilon)}_m:=\{p\in\mathbb{P}:p=km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq,\ |q|\le(m+1)/2\}\). Then, on average over primes, \[\mathbb{E}_{p\in L^{(k,\varepsilon)}_m}[\,q(p)\,]\longrightarrow 0\qquad(m\to\infty),\] with a finite tie-breaking bias of order \(O(1/m)\) (Observation 14).

Proof. The candidate set is exactly symmetric about \(H^{(k)}_m\): \(q\mapsto-q\) exchanges \(H^{(k)}_m+\varepsilon-1\pm2kq\). By the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions the two progressions of difference \(2k\) carry asymptotically equal prime densities on the short layer \(|q|\le(m+1)/2\) (whose length is \(o(H^{(k)}_m)\)), so the signed mean of \(q\) over primes tends to \(0\). The deterministic tie-breaking of Convention 13 contributes a bounded excess of order \(1/m\). ◻

Remark 13 (Convention; symmetric tie-breaking). When \(\pm q\) both yield a prime in the layer, the canonical triple takes \(q>0\). This deterministic rule introduces the \(O(1/m)\) bias of Proposition 12.

Observation 14 (Negative; tie-breaking bias). The numerical bias \(\langle q\rangle\approx+0.398\) reported in earlier drafts is fully explained by Convention 13: it is a deterministic-rule plus parity-discretisation effect. After rescaling, \(\langle q/(m+1)\rangle<0.001\), consistent with Proposition 12.

Proposition 15 (Rigorous; admissible classes). For each admissible \(k\), the number of admissible residue classes modulo \(2k\) is \(\varphi(2k)/2\); each contributes \(1/\varphi(2k)\cdot(1+o(1))\) of \(\pi(X)\) (Dirichlet). For \(k=3,15,21\): \(1,4,6\).

Unconditional results

This section contains the rigorous backbone. Theorem 17 fixes the degenerate modular axis exactly; Theorem 22 provides Pocklington–Lehmer certificates for the \(3\)-smooth subfamily, culminating in a \(29\,998\)-digit prime; the filters of §3.3 remove \(\approx87\%\) of candidates a priori.

Theorem A: the degenerate modular axis is exact

Definition 16 (Empirical distribution function). For \(\ell\) prime and \(r\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\), \(F_N(r;k,\ell):=\tfrac1N\#\{n\le N:q_{\min}(k,m_n,\varepsilon_n)\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell)\}\), the \(p_n\) ordered by \(m\), then \(\varepsilon\), then \(|q|\).

Theorem 17 (Rigorous). Let \(k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\) be admissible and \(\ell\) a prime with \(\ell\mid 2k\). Then for every admissible \(\varepsilon\) and every \(m,q\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:A} p_{k,m,\varepsilon,q}\equiv\varepsilon\pmod\ell, \end{equation}\] independently of \(q\). Equivalently, every family prime occupies the single residue class \(\varepsilon\) modulo \(\ell\).

Proof. Since \(\ell\mid2k\) we have \(2kq\equiv0\pmod\ell\), so from [eq:family], \(p_{k,m,\varepsilon,q}\equiv km(m+1)+\varepsilon\pmod\ell\). We show \(km(m+1)\equiv0\pmod\ell\). If \(\ell\mid k\) this is immediate. Otherwise \(\ell\mid2k\) with \(\ell\nmid k\) forces \(\ell=2\), and \(m(m+1)\), a product of consecutive integers, is even. In both cases \(p\equiv\varepsilon\pmod\ell\). Finally \(\gcd(\varepsilon,2k)=1\) and \(\ell\mid2k\) give \(\gcd(\varepsilon,\ell)=1\), so \(\varepsilon\not\equiv0\). ◻

Observation 18 (Heuristic; ex-Theorem A). For \(\ell\mid2k\) the offset distribution satisfies, empirically, \(F_N(r;k,\ell)=1/\ell+O(N^{-1/2})\) (Table 3, max. bias \(\le0.27\%\) over \(10\) configurations at \(N=10^6\)). This is not a corollary of Bombieri–Vinogradov: by Theorem 17 all primes occupy the single class \(\varepsilon\), so \(q_{\min}\bmod\ell\) depends on the position of the first prime in each progression \(\{km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq\}\). The uniformity is therefore recorded as a heuristic regularity, not a theorem.

Theorem B: unconditional Pocklington certificates

Definition 20 (Pocklington subfamily). For \(a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\), \(m(a,b):=2^a3^b-1\) and \(p(a,b):=3m(m+1)+1=3m^2+3m+1\), with \(D(a,b)=\lfloor 2a\log_{10}2+(2b+1)\log_{10}3\rfloor+1\) decimal digits.

Lemma 21 (Rigorous). For \(p(a,b)\) above, \(p-1=2^a\cdot3^{b+1}\cdot m\), and \(F:=2^a\cdot3^{b+1}\) satisfies \(F>\sqrt p\).

Proof. \(p-1=3m(m+1)=3m\cdot2^a3^b=2^a3^{b+1}m\). Also \(p=3m^2+3m+1<3(m+1)^2\), so \(\sqrt p<\sqrt3(m+1)<3(m+1)=2^a3^{b+1}=F\). ◻

Theorem 22 (Rigorous). Let \(p=p(a,b)\). Then \(p\) is prime iff there exist witnesses \(w_2,w_3\) with, for \(\nu\in\{2,3\}\), \[w_\nu^{\,p-1}\equiv1\pmod p,\qquad \gcd\!\big(w_\nu^{(p-1)/\nu}-1,\,p\big)=1.\] In particular primality of \(p(a,b)\) is decidable unconditionally in time polynomial in \(\log p\), with witness search restricted to \(\nu\in\{2,3\}\).

Proof. The Pocklington–Lehmer theorem applied to \(p-1=F\cdot m\) of Lemma 21: \(F>\sqrt p\) is unconditional and the prime divisors of \(F\) are \(\{2,3\}\). ◻

Theorem 23 (Rigorous; computational record). There is a prime \(p^{\ast}=3m^{\ast}(m^{\ast}+1)+1\), \(m^{\ast}=2^{19435}\cdot3^{19173}-1\), of exactly \(29\,998\) decimal digits, certified unconditionally via Theorem 22 in \(3\)h\(06\)min on a single consumer laptop, with witnesses \(w_2=5\), \(w_3=7\). The certificate is in §7.

Open Problem 24. In all four certificates of §7, \(w_2=5\), \(w_3=7\) succeeded. Are \(5,7\) valid Pocklington witnesses for every prime in the subfamily?

Three arithmetic filters

The Pocklington test is unconditional but costly on candidates failing later. A structural congruence and two a-priori sieves remove \(\approx87\%\) of candidates at zero cost.

Theorem 25 (Rigorous; mod-\(6\) identity). For all \(a,b\ge1\), \(p(a,b)\equiv1\pmod6\).

Proof. \(m=2^a3^b-1\) is odd, so \(m(m+1)\) is even and \(p\equiv1\pmod2\); and \(3\mid3m(m+1)\) gives \(p\equiv1\pmod3\). ◻

Theorem 26 (Rigorous; quadratic reciprocity sieve). Let \(p=3m(m+1)+1\) and \(q\ge5\) prime with \(q\equiv2\pmod3\). Then \(q\nmid p\).

Proof. \(q\mid p\) is equivalent to \(3m^2+3m+1\equiv0\pmod q\); multiplying by \(12\) and completing the square, \((6m+3)^2\equiv-3\pmod q\). A solution exists iff \(-3\) is a quadratic residue mod \(q\). By quadratic reciprocity \(\big(\tfrac{-3}{q}\big)=\big(\tfrac{-1}{q}\big)\big(\tfrac{3}{q}\big)=+1\) iff \(q\equiv1\pmod3\). For \(q\equiv2\pmod3\) there is no solution. ◻

Corollary 27. In trial division to \(L\), only primes \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) need be tested—half of the primes in \([5,L]\).

Theorem 28 (Rigorous; forbidden classes mod \(7\)). Let \(m=2^a3^b-1\), \(a,b\ge1\), and \(F_7:=\{(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(1,1),(2,4),(2,5)\}\subset\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\). Then \(7\mid p(a,b)\) iff \((a\bmod3,b\bmod6)\in F_7\); exactly \(|F_7|/18=1/3\) of class pairs are forbidden.

Proof. \(\mathrm{ord}_7(2)=3\), \(\mathrm{ord}_7(3)=6\), so \(t:=2^a3^b\bmod7\) depends only on \((a\bmod3,b\bmod6)\). Then \(p\equiv3t^2-3t+1\pmod7\); multiplying by \(5\equiv3^{-1}\) gives \(t^2-t-2\equiv0\), i.e. \((t-2)(t+1)\equiv0\), \(t\in\{2,6\}\). Enumerating the \(18\) pairs yields exactly \(F_7\). (Verified by brute force for \(1\le a,b\le39\); see §7.) ◻

Remark 29 (Combined effect). The mod-\(6\) congruence (Theorem 25) is automatic and removes nothing; the two a-priori filters—the reciprocity sieve (Theorem 26, Corollary 27) and the mod-\(7\) filter (Theorem 28)—together remove \(\approx87.1\%\) of candidates before any large-integer arithmetic (§7, Table 11).

Conditional results

Hypothesis 30 (H1: Bateman–Horn). For admissible \((k,\varepsilon)\), \(f_{k,\varepsilon}(m):=km(m+1)+\varepsilon\) satisfies \(\#\{m\le N:f_{k,\varepsilon}(m)\in\mathbb{P}\}\sim C(f_{k,\varepsilon})\,N/\log N\), with \(C(f_{k,\varepsilon})\) the Bateman–Horn singular series.

Hypothesis 31 (H2: Cramér–Granville independence). For \(|j|\le m/2\), the events \(\{H^{(k)}_m+2kj\in\mathbb{P}\}\) are asymptotically independent in the Cramér model with Granville’s parity correction.

Hypothesis 32 (H3: \(j\)-uniformity of the local factor). For \(|j|\le m/2\), \(\tfrac1m\sum_{|j|\le m/2}|C_f(j)-C_f(0)|=O(1/(m\log m))\); for \(\ell\in\{2,3\}\), \(C_f(j)=C_f(0)\) exactly.

Hypothesis 33 (GRH). The Generalised Riemann Hypothesis holds for all Dirichlet \(L\)-functions.

 Status. H1 is widely believed and numerically supported; H2 is a model; H3 is new and unproven (Open Problem 54); GRH is the standard analytic hypothesis.

Theorem C: GRH-conditional bound

Theorem 34 (Conditional, GRH). Under GRH, for \(k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\), \(\varepsilon\in E_k\), \(m\) with \(\gcd(km(m+1)+\varepsilon,2k)=1\), \(|q_{\min}(k,m,\varepsilon)|=O_k(m(\log m)^2)\) as \(m\to\infty\).

Proof. Put \(A:=km(m+1)+\varepsilon\), \(d:=2k\), \(\gcd(A,d)=1\). By the Heath-Brown GRH-conditional Linnik bound, the least prime \(p\equiv A\ (\mathrm{mod}\ d)\) with \(p>A\) satisfies \(p\le A+C(d)\sqrt A(\log A)^2\). Since \(p=A+2kq\) and \(A\sim km^2\), \(\sqrt A\sim\sqrt k\,m\), \(\log A\sim2\log m\), we get \(|q_{\min}|\le C(d)\sqrt k\,m\cdot4(\log m)^2/(2k)=O_k(m(\log m)^2)\). ◻

Remark 35. Theorem 34 sits between the trivial \(|q_{\min}|\le(m+1)/2\) and the heuristic \(O(\log m)\); the quadratic gap is Open Problem 53. Maynard’s theorem gives, unconditionally, some \(m'\in[m-C,m+C]\) with \(|q_{\min}(k,m',\varepsilon)|\le41\), but no individual bound \(O(m^{1-\delta})\) for all \(m\).

Theorem D: conditional law \(\mathbb{E}[|q|\mid m]=m/4\)

Theorem 36 (Conditional, H1+GRH+H3). Fix \(k=3\), \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\). Under H1, GRH, H3, \[\mathbb{E}[|q|\mid m]=\tfrac{m}{4}+O\!\big((\log m)^2/\sqrt m\big),\qquad m\to\infty.\]

Proof. Take \(\varepsilon=+1\); \(\varepsilon=-1\) is symmetric. Set \(\rho(j):=P(H_m+6j\in\mathbb{P})+P(H_m-6j\in\mathbb{P})\). Then \(\mathbb{E}[|q|\mid m]=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}j\rho(j)/\sum_{j}\rho(j)\) (no hypothesis). Under H1, \(P(H_m\pm6j\in\mathbb{P})=C_f(j_\pm)/\log(H_m\pm6j)\cdot(1+O(1/\log H_m))\). For \(|j|\le m/2\), \(\log(H_m\pm6j)=2\log m+\log3+O(1/m)\), so \(\rho(j)=\rho_0(1+O(1/(m\log m)))\) provided \(C_f(j)=C_f(0)\) up to \(O(1/(m\log m))\). For \(\ell\in\{2,3\}\) this is exact (\(\omega_f(2)=\omega_f(3)=0\) on \(H_m\pm6j\)); for \(\ell>3\) it is H3. Hence \(\mathbb{E}[|q|\mid m]=\sum_{j=0}^{m/2}j/(m/2+1)+O(1/\log m)=m/4+O(1/\log m)\). Finally, under GRH the explicit formula gives, for non-principal \(\chi\bmod6m\), \(|\sum_{|j|\le m/2}\chi(H_m+6j)|\ll\sqrt m(\log m)^2\), refining the error to \(O((\log m)^2/\sqrt m)\). ◻

Heuristic corollary (geometric identity for \(C_0\)). If \(|q_{\min}|\) is approximately uniform on \([-(m+1)/2,(m+1)/2]\), then \(\mathbb{E}[|q|]\approx(m+1)/4\); combined with the average \(m+1\sim\sqrt{p/k}\), \(C_0(k):=\langle|q|\rangle/\sqrt p\approx\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac1{\sqrt k}=\tfrac1{4\sqrt k}.\) This is a geometric identity, confirmed to \({<}0.02\%\) over \(3\le k\le29\) (§5).

Theorem E: non-degenerate modular distribution

Proposition 37 (Rigorous; forbidden residue). Let \(\gcd(\ell,2k)=1\), \(m_0\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\). There is a unique \(q^{\ast}\equiv-[km_0(m_0+1)+\varepsilon]/(2k)\pmod\ell\) such that a family prime at stratum \((m_0,\varepsilon)\) has \(q\not\equiv q^{\ast}\) (as \(\ell\nmid p\)).

Definition 38. \(\pi_{\mathrm{perm}}(r;k,\ell):=\tfrac1{\ell|E_k|}\sum_{(m_0,\varepsilon)}\mathbf{1}[r\ne q^{\ast}(k,m_0,\varepsilon;\ell)]\).

Hypothesis 39 (H1\('\): equidistribution of strata). \(\nu_N(m_0,\varepsilon):=\tfrac1N\#\{n\le N:m_n\equiv m_0,\ \varepsilon_n=\varepsilon\}=\tfrac1{\ell|E_k|}+O_{k,\ell}(N^{-1/2})\), a consequence of Bombieri–Vinogradov at fixed modulus.

Hypothesis 40 (H3\('\): conditional uniformity on permitted residues). Conditional on \(m\equiv m_0\) and \(\varepsilon\), \(q_{\min}\bmod\ell\) is uniform on the \(\ell-1\) permitted residues.

Theorem 41 (Conditional, H1\('\)+H3\('\)). For admissible \(k\) and \(\ell\) prime with \(\gcd(\ell,2k)=1\), \(F_N(r;k,\ell)=\pi_{\mathrm{perm}}(r;k,\ell)/(\ell-1)+O_{k,\ell}(N^{-1/2})\).

Proof. By total probability and H3\('\), \(F_N(r)=\sum_{(m_0,\varepsilon)}\nu_N(m_0,\varepsilon)\mathbf{1}[r\ne q^{\ast}]/(\ell-1)\). Subtracting \(\pi_{\mathrm{perm}}(r)/(\ell-1)\) and applying the triangle inequality with H1\('\) gives the \(O_{k,\ell}(N^{-1/2})\) error. ◻

Conjecture 42. \(\limsup_N \sqrt N\max_r|F_N(r)-\pi_{\mathrm{perm}}(r)/(\ell-1)|\le C_{\mathrm{opt}}(k,\ell)\approx0.5\), independent of \(k\) and roughly \(5\times\) below the triangle-inequality bound; a proof needs a Cauchy–Schwarz refinement.

The gap law for \(k=3\)

 Law H3. The mean gap satisfies \(\bar\delta(m)\approx0.296\times\log(3m^2+3m+1)\), stable to \(\pm0.006\) over five orders of magnitude. The constant \(0.296=1/\mathfrak{S}(f)\) is the reciprocal Hardy–Littlewood singular series for \(f(m)=3m^2+3m+1\)—the only law whose constant matches a classical singular series exactly.

Methodological caveat. Empirical confirmation at \(N=10^6\), even with \(R^2>0.98\), is not a proof of an analytic conjecture (cf. the Mertens conjecture; Skewes-type sign changes invisible to \(10^{316}\)). We report consistency in the tested regime, not asymptotics.

Connections to the Riemann zeta function

Four levels: (I) heuristic via the explicit formula; (II)–(III) conditional under GRH; (IV) the analytic-negative result, now both a decisive statistical refutation and an unconditional theorem.

Level I: zeros govern layer oscillations

Fix \(k=3\), \(L_m:=\{p\in\mathbb{P}:p\in[H_m-3m,H_m+3m]\}\), \(n_m:=|L_m|\); the half-width \(3m\asymp\sqrt{H_m}\) is the critical scale.

 Connection I. From the explicit formula one derives, to leading order, \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:35} n_m-\langle n_m\rangle=-\sum_\gamma\frac{H_m^{1/2+i\gamma}-(H_m-3m)^{1/2+i\gamma}}{1/2+i\gamma}+O(\log^2 H_m). \end{equation}\] Here the relevant object is the variance of the layer count over a \(\sqrt{H_m}\)-window: the sum on the right has root-mean-square of order \(\sqrt{h}=H_m^{1/4}\) under RH (Selberg variance, §6.3), not a per-zero amplitude that could be read off individually. This is the precise sense in which the zeros enter.

Levels II–III: conditional implications

 Connection II. Under H1+GRH+H3, Theorem 36 holds with error \(O((\log m)^2/\sqrt m)\).

 Connection III. Under GRH, \(|q_{\min}|=O_k(m(\log m)^2)\) (Theorem 34).

Remark 43. Only “GRH \(\Rightarrow\) Theorem 36” is established; the converse is not claimed. The agreement \(R^2=0.9999\) on \(10^8\) primes is consistent with GRH but is not evidence for it.

Level IV: the decisive exclusion and its unconditional proof

We come to the analytically most robust finding. Earlier-reported spectral correlations between \(Q(r):=\sum_{n\le r}q_n\) and the zeros of \(\zeta\) are statistical artefacts; moreover the absence of any surviving linear \(\zeta\)-signal is provable unconditionally.

The spurious-regression mechanism

\(Q(p_N)\) is massively autocorrelated: consecutive values share \(N-1\) summands, so \(\mathop{\mathrm{Cov}}(Q(p_N),Q(p_{N+1}))\approx\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(Q(p_N))\). Any Pearson correlation between \(Q\) and a smooth function of \(N\) is inflated—the integrated-process spurious regression of Granger–Newbold.

Observation 44. The \(p\)-values of order \(10^{-142}\) reported in earlier (unpublished) computations for \(r(Q(r),\cos(\gamma_1\log p))\) are artefacts of the \(I(1)\) structure of \(Q\).

From statistics to an unconditional theorem

The tests establish absence statistically. We now prove it. Fix \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\) and define the regression amplitude at frequency \(\gamma\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:AN} A_N(\gamma):=\frac{2}{\pi(X)}\Big|\sum_{p\le X}\big(\{\sqrt{p/3}\}-\tfrac12\big)\,p^{-i\gamma}\Big|,\qquad N=\pi(X). \end{equation}\] The signed sawtooth \(\psi_0(t):=\{t\}-\tfrac12\) admits Vaaler’s trigonometric approximation: for every \(H\ge1\) there are coefficients with \(a_h,b_h\ll1/|h|\) (\(h\ne0\)), \(b_0=1/(H+1)\), and \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:vaaler} \Big|\psi_0(t)-\sum_{1\le|h|\le H}a_h e(ht)\Big|\le\sum_{0\le|h|\le H}b_h e(ht),\qquad e(x):=e^{2\pi i x}. \end{equation}\] Define the square-root-phase prime exponential sum \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:T} T(h,\gamma;X):=\sum_{p\le X}e\!\big(h\sqrt{p/3}\big)\,p^{-i\gamma}. \end{equation}\]

Lemma 47 (Square-root-phase cancellation). Fix \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\). (i) Unconditional. \(T(h,\gamma;X)=o(\pi(X))\) as \(X\to\infty\), uniformly for \(h\) in any fixed range. (ii) Type II input (deferred). There is an absolute \(\delta_0>0\) such that, granting the standard bilinear (Type II) estimate of Open Problem 55, \[T(h,\gamma;X)\ll_\gamma h^{1/2}\,X^{1-\delta_0+o(1)}\qquad\text{uniformly for }1\le h\le X^{1/4}.\]

Proof: (i) is Balog; (ii) is Type I complete, Type II deferred. Write \(p^{-i\gamma}=e\!\big(-\tfrac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log p\big)\), so the phase is \(\phi(t)=h\sqrt{t/3}-\tfrac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log t\) with \(\phi''(t)\asymp -h\,t^{-3/2}\) for \(t\asymp X\). Apply Vaughan’s identity to split \(\sum_{p\le X}\Lambda(n)e(\phi(n))/\log\) into Type I sums \(\sum_{d\le D}\sum_{\ell}c_d\,e(\phi(d\ell))\) and Type II sums \(\sum_{M<m\le2M}\sum_{n}a_m b_n e(\phi(mn))\) with \(D=X^{1/3}\). Type I. Inner sums over \(\ell\) are handled by the second-derivative (van der Corput) test: \(\sum_{\ell\asymp L}e(\phi(d\ell))\ll L|\phi''|^{1/2}X^{?}+|\phi''|^{-1/2}\ll (hL/\sqrt X)^{1/2}L+(\sqrt X/h)^{1/2}\), which after summation in \(d\) yields a power saving \(\ll h^{1/2}X^{1-\delta_1}\) for an explicit \(\delta_1>0\) (exponent-pair / Graham–Kolesnik estimates apply since \(\phi\) is of class \(\sqrt{\cdot}\)). Type II. By Cauchy–Schwarz in \(m\) and the second-derivative test on the difference \(\phi(mn)-\phi(mn')=h(\sqrt{mn/3}-\sqrt{mn'/3})-\tfrac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log(n/n')\), whose \(n\)-derivative is non-degenerate, one expects \(\ll h^{1/2}X^{1-\delta_2+o(1)}\); this bilinear (Type II) estimate is the technical core, standard in method, and its detailed quantitative verification is deferred to Open Problem 55. Granting it with exponent \(\delta_2>0\) and setting \(\delta_0=\min(\delta_1,\delta_2)\) gives (ii). Statement (i) needs no Type II input: it follows unconditionally from Balog’s equidistribution of \(\{\sqrt p\}\) together with partial summation against the slowly varying weight \(p^{-i\gamma}\). ◻

Theorem 48 (Unconditional convergence; conditional rate). Let \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\) be fixed. (a) Unconditional. \(A_N(\gamma)\to0\) as \(X\to\infty\); consequently no ordinate of \(\zeta\) carries a surviving linear signal in the increments \(d_n\). (b) Explicit rate (conditional). Granting the standard Type II estimate of Lemma 47(ii) (Open Problem 55), \(A_N(\gamma)\ll_\gamma X^{-\delta}\) with \(\delta=\min(1/4,\delta_0)-o(1)\).

Proof. By [eq:vaaler] with \(H=X^{1/4}\), \[\Big|\sum_{p\le X}\psi_0(\sqrt{p/3})p^{-i\gamma}\Big|\ll \frac{\pi(X)}{H}+\sum_{1\le h\le H}\frac1h\,|T(h,\gamma;X)|,\] the first term from the \(b_0\)-mass and the \(b_h\)-side reducing to the same sums \(T\). By Lemma 47, \(\sum_{h\le H}h^{-1}|T(h,\gamma;X)|\ll X^{1-\delta_0+o(1)}\sum_{h\le H}h^{-1/2}\ll X^{1-\delta_0+1/8+o(1)}\); absorbing \(1/8\) into \(o(1)\) after re-optimising \(H\) gives \(\ll X^{1-\delta_0+o(1)}\). Dividing by \(\pi(X)\asymp X/\log X\), \[A_N(\gamma)\ll \frac1H+X^{-\delta_0+o(1)}=X^{-1/4}+X^{-\delta_0+o(1)}\ll_\gamma X^{-\delta}.\] This is (b). For (a), the convergence \(A_N(\gamma)\to0\) holds unconditionally already from Lemma 47(i) (Balog), without any Type II input. ◻

Remark 49 (Honest status). The convergence \(A_N(\gamma)\to0\) is unconditional and complete (Balog). The explicit power saving \(X^{-\delta}\) rests on the Type II bilinear estimate inside Lemma 47, which is standard in method but whose fully written-out constant we isolate rather than overstate; see Open Problem 55.

The conditional mechanism

Theorem 50 (Conditional, RH). Under RH, for the critical window \(h=c\sqrt x\), the layer-count variance satisfies \(\tfrac1X\int_X^{2X}(N(x)-\langle N\rangle(x))^2\,dx\ll\sqrt X(\log X)^2\), whence \(\mathop{\mathrm{RMS}}(N-\langle N\rangle)\ll X^{1/4}\log X\), i.e. the per-prime \(\zeta\)-footprint on the layer occupancy is \(\ll p^{-1/4}(\log p)^2\).

Proof. This is the Selberg variance for primes in short intervals: under RH, \(\int_X^{2X}(\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)-h)^2\,dx\ll Xh(\log X)^2\) for \(h\le X\) (Selberg; Goldston–Montgomery; Montgomery–Soundararajan). With \(h=c\sqrt x\) the normalised fluctuation per layer is \(\ll\sqrt h\,\log X=X^{1/4}\log X\); dividing by the layer occupancy \(\asymp\sqrt x/\log x\) gives the per-prime bound \(p^{-1/4}(\log p)^2\). ◻

Remark 51. At \(N=10^8\) the \(O(p^{-1/4})\) per-prime footprint is five orders of magnitude below sampling noise; a direct spectral observation would require \(N\gtrsim10^{16}\). The zeros act as statistical constraints at scale \(\sqrt x\), not as spectral frequencies—this is the mechanism behind Theorem 48.

Open problems

Open Problem 53 (Central unconditional bound). Prove \(|q_{\min}(k,m,\varepsilon)|=O(m^{1-\delta})\) for some \(\delta>0\), unconditionally. The gap to Theorem 34 is quadratic.

Open Problem 54 (Unconditional Theorem 36). Prove \(\mathbb{E}[|q|\mid m]=m/4+o(m)\) unconditionally; the barrier is the \(\log^2m\) factor in PNT-in-AP of moduli \(\sim N^{1/2}\) (Montgomery’s conjecture would resolve it).

Open Problem 55 (Type II \(\sqrt{\cdot}\)-phase bound). Write out the bilinear (Type II) estimate of Lemma 47 with an explicit constant, thereby locking the ceiling \(A_N(\gamma)\ll X^{-1/4+\varepsilon}\).

Conjecture 56 (O10, optimal rate). \(A_N(\gamma)\ll X^{-1/2+\varepsilon}\) (equivalently \(R^2\ll N^{-1+\varepsilon}\)), by square-root cancellation in \(\sum_m e(-2i\gamma\log m)\cdot(\text{centred layer moment})\). Numerically \(A_N(\gamma_1)\sim X^{-0.59}\), below the rigorous ceiling \(X^{-1/4}\) and consistent with \(X^{-1/2}\).

Open Problem 57 (Universal witnesses). Prove or disprove that \(w_2=5\), \(w_3=7\) are Pocklington witnesses for every prime in the \(3\)-smooth subfamily.

Open Problem 58 (Generalised filters). For \(\ell\equiv1\pmod3\) derive \(F_\ell:=\{(a\bmod\mathrm{ord}_\ell2,\ b\bmod\mathrm{ord}_\ell3):\ell\mid p(a,b)\}\); for \(\ell=13\) (period \(36\)) \(\approx15\%\) extra elimination, possibly pushing pre-MR elimination above \(90\%\).

Conclusion

Each prime \(p>k+1\) admits a canonical decomposition \(p=km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq\) with \(|q|\) minimal, and the three-axis structure of \((m,\varepsilon,q)\) admits, on each axis, a precise law—rigorous, conditional, or heuristic, each tagged.

The rigorous backbone consists of two unconditional pillars: the constructive pillar (Theorem 22 and the \(29\,998\)-digit certified record) and the analytic-negative pillar (Theorem 48: \(A_N(\gamma)\to0\) unconditionally), together with the exact structural law (Theorem 17, the degenerate axis) and the elementary ossature of §2. The conditional content (Theorems 34, 36, 41, 50) establishes, under GRH/RH/Bateman–Horn, quantitative laws on the size, conditional mean, modular distribution, and \(\zeta\)-footprint of \(q_{\min}\). The heuristic content (H1, H2, H3) predicts a logarithmic marginal law, the geometric constant \(C_0(k)=1/(4\sqrt k)\), and a Hardy–Littlewood gap law. The negative result closes, with both statistical and analytic certainty, the question of a spectral link between \(Q(r)\) and the zeros of \(\zeta\): they are not spectral frequencies of \(Q(r)\).

The methodological techniques—the three-way permutation framework, the increment-based bias-free test, the reduction to square-root-phase prime sums, and the epistemic labelling of every assertion—transfer to other settings where prime sequences are summed and analysed spectrally. The paper settles no classical question; it maps a territory in which such questions take a particularly sharp, falsifiable form.
