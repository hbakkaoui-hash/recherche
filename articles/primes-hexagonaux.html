Certificats de primalité inconditionnels pour la famille hexagonale 3-lisse $p=3m(m+1)+1$

Hassane Bakkaoui · Chercheur indépendant

PDF arXiv

All quantitative claims are labelled rigorous (unconditional, proved) or computational (verified by exact arithmetic over an explicit finite range). The two witness theorems and the three filters are rigorous. The \(29\,998\)-digit prime is a genuine, independently re-verified certificate, but it is not a record by general standards (general-purpose ECPP reaches comparable sizes, and \(N-1\) provable primes of special form reach millions of digits); we report it only as a demonstration of the framework on commodity hardware, and as the largest certificate within this subfamily. The paper resolves no classical open problem.

Introduction

Proving unconditionally that a given integer is prime is a central task of computational number theory. For generic large primes, the most practical route is the Pocklington–Lehmer criterion : exhibit a fully factored divisor \(F>\sqrt p\) of \(p-1\) and verify witnesses. The key difficulty is finding such an \(F\).

The subfamily \[p=3m(m+1)+1,\qquad m=2^{a}3^{b}-1,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\] is constructed so that \(m+1=2^{a}3^{b}\) is \(3\)-smooth by design, making \(F=2^{a}3^{b+1}\) fully factored and satisfying \(F>\sqrt p\) for every \((a,b)\) without any condition on \(p\). Equivalently, \(p=3m^2+3m+1=(m+1)^3-m^3\) is a centred hexagonal number, and the family is the locus where its \(N-1\) certificate is unconditional and minimal.

Contributions

This paper makes two kinds of contribution.

(A) Deterministic Pocklington witnesses (rigorous, new). In every certificate produced by the \(N-1\) method one must exhibit, for each prime \(q\mid F\), a witness \(w_q\) with \(w_q^{(p-1)/q}\not\equiv 1\). Here \(q\in\{2,3\}\). Earlier computations used the fixed witnesses \(w_2=5,\ w_3=7\) and observed that they always worked; whether this holds for the whole subfamily was left open. We settle it: \(w_2=5\) and \(w_3=7\) are not universal, but their validity is governed by exact congruences in \((a,b)\):

  • Theorem 10: \(w_2=5\) is valid \(\iff a-b\equiv 1,2\pmod 4\) (quadratic reciprocity);

  • Theorem 14: \(w_3=7\) is valid \(\iff m\not\equiv 2\pmod 7\) (cubic reciprocity).

These give a deterministic witness-selection rule (Corollary 18), making the search provably correct rather than reliant on the luck of fixed witnesses.

(B) Arithmetic filters and a computational demonstration. Three elementary congruence theorems (§3) remove \(\approx 87\%\) of candidate pairs \((a,b)\) before any large-number arithmetic. A multi-core implementation (§5–§6) produced four unconditional certificates, the largest of \(29\,998\) digits, on a consumer laptop.

Position

This note is a follow-up to , which develops the heuristic and conditional analytic theory of the full family \(p=km(m+1)+\varepsilon+2kq\). The present paper isolates the structurally distinguished \(3\)-smooth subfamily (\(\varepsilon=+1\), \(q=0\), \(m+1=2^a3^b\)) on which the Pocklington method, and now also the witness selection, become unconditional and explicit. This is a short note: its genuinely new content is the deterministic witness characterisation of §4 (Theorems 10 and 14, Corollary 18); the arithmetic filters (§3) and the certificate (§6) recall and specialise material from , and are included for self-containedness.

The Pocklington subfamily

Definition 1. For integers \(a,b\ge 1\), set \(m(a,b)=2^{a}3^{b}-1\) and \(p(a,b)=3m(m+1)+1=3m^2+3m+1\). Its decimal digit count is \(D(a,b)=\lfloor 2a\log_{10}2+(2b+1)\log_{10}3\rfloor+1\).

Lemma 2 (Explicit factorisation). For \(p\) as in Definition 1, \[p-1=2^{a}3^{b+1}\,m,\qquad F:=2^{a}3^{b+1}>\sqrt p.\]

Proof. Since \(m+1=2^{a}3^{b}\), \(p-1=3m(m+1)=3m\cdot 2^{a}3^{b}=2^{a}3^{b+1}m\). As \(p=3m^2+3m+1<3(m+1)^2\), we get \(\sqrt p<\sqrt3\,(m+1)<3(m+1)=2^{a}3^{b+1}=F\). ◻

Theorem 3 (Pocklington–Lehmer certificate ). Let \(p=p(a,b)\). Then \(p\) is prime iff there exist \(w_2,w_3\) such that, for \(q\in\{2,3\}\), \[w_q^{\,p-1}\equiv 1\pmod p,\qquad \gcd\!\bigl(w_q^{(p-1)/q}-1,\,p\bigr)=1.\]

Corollary 4. By Lemma 2, \(F>\sqrt p\) holds unconditionally and the only primes dividing \(F\) are \(2,3\); hence the certificate needs at most the two witnesses \(w_2,w_3\).

For \(p\) prime, \(w_q^{p-1}\equiv1\) is automatic (Fermat), and the second condition is equivalent to \(w_q^{(p-1)/q}\not\equiv1\pmod p\). Since (Theorem 5) \(p\equiv1\pmod6\), both \((p-1)/2\) and \((p-1)/3\) are integers, and \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:wit} w_2\text{ valid}\iff w_2\text{ is a quadratic non-residue mod }p,\qquad w_3\text{ valid}\iff w_3\text{ is a cubic non-residue mod }p. \end{equation}\]

Three arithmetic filters

Theorem 5 (mod \(6\)). For all \(a,b\ge1\), \(p(a,b)\equiv1\pmod6\).

Proof. \(m=2^{a}3^{b}-1\) is odd, so \(m(m+1)\) is even and \(p=3m(m+1)+1\) is odd; and \(3\mid 3m(m+1)\) gives \(p\equiv1\pmod3\). By CRT, \(p\equiv1\pmod6\). ◻

Theorem 6 (\((-3)\)-sieve; quadratic reciprocity). Let \(m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\), \(p=3m(m+1)+1\), and \(q\ge5\) a prime with \(q\equiv2\pmod3\). Then \(q\nmid p\).

Proof. \(q\mid p\) is equivalent to \(3m^2+3m+1\equiv0\pmod q\); completing the square, \((6m+3)^2\equiv-3\pmod q\), so \(-3\) must be a quadratic residue mod \(q\). By quadratic reciprocity , \(\left(\frac{-3}{q}\right)=+1\) iff \(q\equiv1\pmod3\). For \(q\equiv2\pmod3\) there is no solution, so \(q\nmid p\). ◻

Corollary 7. In trial division up to \(L\), only primes \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) need be tested, i.e. half of the primes in \([5,L]\) by Dirichlet.

Theorem 8 (Forbidden classes mod \(7\)). Let \(m=2^{a}3^{b}-1\). With \(\mathcal F_7=\{(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(1,1),(2,4),(2,5)\}\subset \mathbb{Z}/3\times\mathbb{Z}/6\), \[7\mid p(a,b)\iff (a\bmod3,\,b\bmod6)\in\mathcal F_7 ,\] exactly \(1/3\) of all residue classes.

Proof. Since \(\mathrm{ord}_7(2)=3\) and \(\mathrm{ord}_7(3)=6\), \(t:=2^{a}3^{b}\bmod7\) depends only on \((a\bmod3,b\bmod6)\), and \(m\equiv t-1\), \(p\equiv3t^2-3t+1\pmod7\). Multiplying \(7\mid p\) by \(5\equiv3^{-1}\) gives \(t^2-t-2\equiv0\), i.e. \((t-2)(t+1)\equiv0\), so \(t\in\{2,6\}\). Enumerating the \(18\) values of \(t\) (Table 1) yields exactly \(\mathcal F_7\). ◻

Deterministic Pocklington witnesses

This section is the theoretical core. By [eq:wit], the validity of a fixed witness is a residuosity question. We resolve both witnesses exactly.

The witness \(w_2=5\)

Theorem 10 (\(w_2=5\); rigorous). For \(p=p(a,b)\) prime, \(w_2=5\) is a valid Pocklington witness if and only if \[m\equiv 1\text{ or }3\pmod5 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a-b\equiv 1\text{ or }2\pmod4 .\]

Proof. By [eq:wit], \(w_2=5\) is valid iff \(\left(\frac{5}{p}\right)=-1\). As \(5\equiv1\pmod4\), quadratic reciprocity gives \(\left(\frac{5}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{p}{5}\right)\), and \(\left(\frac{p}{5}\right)=-1\) iff \(p\equiv2,3\pmod5\). Modulo \(5\), \(p=3m(m+1)+1\) takes the values \(1,2,4,2,1\) for \(m\equiv0,1,2,3,4\); hence \(p\equiv2,3\pmod5\) (the value \(3\) never occurs) iff \(m\equiv1,3\pmod5\). Finally \(m+1=2^{a}3^{b}\) and \(3\equiv2^{3}\pmod5\) give \(m+1\equiv2^{\,a+3b}\pmod5\) with \(\mathrm{ord}_5(2)=4\), so \(m\equiv1\iff a+3b\equiv1\) and \(m\equiv3\iff a+3b\equiv2\pmod4\). Since \(a+3b\equiv a-b\pmod4\), the claim follows. ◻

Remark 11. For \(a-b\equiv0,3\pmod4\), \(5\) is a quadratic residue and \(w_2=5\) fails; the least quadratic non-residue is then a valid witness.

An explicit Eisenstein prime

Lemma 12. Let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\). Then in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\), \[p=(m+1)^3-m^3=\pi\,\overline\pi,\qquad \pi:=(1+m)-m\omega,\qquad N(\pi)=p,\] so \(\pi\) is a prime of \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) above \(p\). Consequently \(7\) is a cubic residue mod \(p\) iff \(\left(\frac{7}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}=1\), where \(\left(\frac{\cdot}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}\) is the cubic residue symbol.

Proof. \(3m^2+3m+1=(m+1)^3-m^3\) (centred hexagonal identity). With \(X^3-Y^3=(X-Y)(X-\omega Y)(X-\omega^2Y)\), \(X=m+1\), \(Y=m\): \(p=\bigl((1+m)-\omega m\bigr)\bigl((1+m)-\omega^2 m\bigr)\). A direct computation gives \(N(\pi)=(1+m)^2-(1+m)(-m)+m^2=1+3m+3m^2=p\); since \(p\equiv1\pmod3\) is prime it splits into two conjugate primes of norm \(p\), and \(\pi\) is one of them. ◻

The witness \(w_3=7\)

The proof rests on the following proposition, which holds for the broader family \(p=3m^2+3m+1\) subject only to \(m\equiv2\pmod3\).

Proposition 13 (Cubic character of \(7\)). Let \(p=3m^2+3m+1\) be prime, \(p\neq7\), with \(m\equiv2\pmod3\). Then \[7\text{ is a cubic residue mod }p\iff m\equiv 2\text{ or }6\pmod7 .\]

Proof. Factor \(7=\lambda\overline\lambda\) with \(\lambda=3+\omega\), \(\overline\lambda=2-\omega\) (\(N(\lambda)=7\)). Their primary associates (those \(\equiv2\bmod3\)) are \(\lambda^{*}=2+3\omega=-\omega^2\lambda\) and \(\overline\lambda^{*}=-1-3\omega=-\omega\,\overline\lambda\), equivalently \(\lambda=-\omega\,\lambda^{*}\) and \(\overline\lambda=-\omega^2\,\overline\lambda^{*}\). Since \(m\equiv2\pmod3\), the primary associate of \(\pi=(1+m)-m\omega\) is \[\pi^{*}=-\omega^{2}\pi=(1+2m)+(1+m)\omega \qquad(1+2m\equiv2,\ 1+m\equiv0\bmod3).\] The cubic symbol depends only on the prime ideal in its lower entry, so \(\left(\frac{7}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{7}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\). By multiplicativity and \(\left(\frac{-1}{\cdot}\right)_{\!3}=1\), \[\left(\frac{7}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3} =\left(\frac{\omega}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\left(\frac{\lambda^{*}}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\cdot \left(\frac{\omega^2}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\left(\frac{\overline\lambda^{*}}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3},\] and \(\left(\frac{\omega}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\left(\frac{\omega^2}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{\omega^3}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}=1\): the units cancel. Eisenstein’s law of cubic reciprocity , applied to the primary primes \(\lambda^{*},\overline\lambda^{*},\pi^{*}\), gives \(\left(\frac{\lambda^{*}}{\pi^{*}}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\) and similarly for \(\overline\lambda^{*}\), so \[\left(\frac{7}{\pi}\right)_{\!3}=\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\cdot\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\overline\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}.\] We evaluate in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]/(\lambda^{*})\cong\mathbb{F}_7\) and \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]/(\overline\lambda^{*})\cong\mathbb{F}_7\). From \(\lambda^{*}=2+3\omega\equiv0\) we get \(\omega\equiv4\pmod7\) (and \(\omega^2\equiv2\)); from \(\overline\lambda^{*}=-1-3\omega\equiv0\), \(\omega\equiv2\) (and \(\omega^2\equiv4\)). Hence \[\pi^{*}\equiv(1+2m)+4(1+m)=5-m \ (\mathrm{mod}\ \lambda^{*}),\qquad \pi^{*}\equiv(1+2m)+2(1+m)=3+4m \ (\mathrm{mod}\ \overline\lambda^{*}).\] Since \(\left(\frac{\alpha}{\theta}\right)_{\!3}\equiv\alpha^{(N(\theta)-1)/3}=\alpha^{2}\pmod\theta\) for \(N(\theta)=7\), we have \(\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\equiv(5-m)^2\) and \(\left(\frac{\pi^{*}}{\overline\lambda^{*}}\right)_{\!3}\equiv(3+4m)^2\) in \(\mathbb{F}_7\), each lying in the cube-roots subgroup \(\{1,2,4\}=\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}\) under the identifications above (\(\omega\mapsto4\) resp. \(\omega\mapsto2\)). Multiplying in \(\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}\), the product equals \(1\) exactly for \(m\equiv2,6\pmod7\); this is the finite verification of Table 2. (The omitted classes \(m\equiv1,5\pmod7\) give \(5-m\equiv0\) or \(3+4m\equiv0\), i.e. \(7\mid p\).) ◻

Theorem 14 (\(w_3=7\); rigorous). For \(p=p(a,b)\) prime, \(w_3=7\) is a valid Pocklington witness if and only if \[m\not\equiv2\pmod7 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad (a\bmod3,b\bmod6)\notin\{(0,1),(1,5),(2,3)\}.\]

Proof. If \(p=7\) then \(m=1\) and \(\pi\) is associate to \(\lambda\), so \(w_3=7\) does not apply; discard this case. For \(p>7\): since \(b\ge1\), \(3\mid3^b\), so \(m+1\equiv0\pmod3\), i.e. \(m\equiv2\pmod3\); and \(m+1=2^{a}3^{b}\) is coprime to \(7\), so \(m\not\equiv6\pmod7\); finally \(m\equiv1,5\pmod7\) give \(7\mid p\) (Theorem 8), excluded. Thus \(m\bmod7\in\{0,2,3,4\}\) and Proposition 13 applies: \(7\) is a cubic residue iff \(m\equiv2\) or \(6\pmod7\), hence (as \(m\not\equiv6\)) iff \(m\equiv2\pmod7\). By [eq:wit], \(w_3=7\) is valid iff \(7\) is a cubic non-residue, i.e. \(m\not\equiv2\pmod7\). The translation to \((a\bmod3,b\bmod6)\) follows from \(m\equiv2\pmod7\iff m+1\equiv3\pmod7\), whose solutions in Table 1 are \(\{(0,1),(1,5),(2,3)\}\). ◻

Remark 15 (Role of \(3\)-smoothness). Proposition 13 distinguishes two residue classes (\(m\equiv2,6\)) and holds for any \(m\equiv2\pmod3\). It is the \(3\)-smoothness \(m+1=2^{a}3^{b}\) that both forces \(m\equiv2\pmod3\) (fixing the primary normalisation of \(\pi\)) and excludes \(m\equiv6\pmod7\), collapsing the criterion to the single congruence \(m\equiv2\pmod7\). Without smoothness, cubic residuosity of \(7\) depends on \(m\bmod21\) (Proposition 16), not \(m\bmod7\) alone.

Remark 17. In the present \(3\)-smooth subfamily one has \(m\equiv2\pmod3\) and \(m\not\equiv6\pmod7\) (by \(3\)-smoothness), so only the class \(m\equiv2\pmod7\) survives, recovering Theorem 14.

Deterministic witness selection

Corollary 18. The fixed pair \((w_2,w_3)=(5,7)\) is not valid for all primes of the subfamily; its validity is the conjunction \(a-b\equiv1,2\pmod4\) and \(m\not\equiv2\pmod7\). A correct witness choice is determined by the residue class of \((a,b)\): \[w_2=\begin{cases}5,& a-b\equiv1,2\ (4),\\ \text{least quadratic non-residue},&\text{else;}\end{cases}\qquad w_3=\begin{cases}7,& m\not\equiv2\ (7),\\ \text{least cubic non-residue},& m\equiv2\ (7).\end{cases}\] In the case \(m\equiv2\pmod7\) a cubic non-residue is found deterministically by testing small primes: cubic non-residues form two-thirds of \((\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}\), so one is reached after a bounded search. Hence the certificate search is provably correct, rather than reliant on fixed witnesses succeeding by chance.

The PrimeQuest algorithm

For target digit count \(D\) the search centre is \(a_0=b_0=\lfloor D/(2\log_{10}6)\rfloor\approx 0.643\,D\), and the zigzag expands outward, enumerating for each \(a\) all \(b\) with \(|D(a,b)-D|\le\tau\) (\(\tau=10\)). Each pair passes through a four-stage pipeline:

  1. mod-\(7\) filter (Theorem 8): reject if \((a\bmod3,b\bmod6)\in\mathcal F_7\). Cost \(O(1)\).

  2. Sieve (Corollary 7): for primes \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), \(q\le L\), reject if \(p\equiv0\).

  3. Miller–Rabin: \(k\) rounds on \(p(a,b)\).

  4. Pocklington: select witnesses by Corollary 18 and verify Theorem 3.

Pairs are distributed over CPU cores; checkpoint-resume every \(60\) s allows exact restart. We note that stage 4 is now deterministic: by Corollary 18 the witnesses are read off \((a,b)\) rather than searched.

Remark 19 (MR round reduction). Miller–Rabin is deterministic for primes (every prime passes every round); reducing the initial round count carries zero risk of discarding a true prime, and rare composites are removed by confirmation rounds and by Pocklington.

Performance and scaling

The dominant cost is Miller–Rabin: one round on a \(D\)-digit modulus costs \(O(D^2\log D\log\log D)\) bit operations via FFT multiplication . Empirically, wall time per MR event scales as \(\tau\propto D^{2.5}\) over the tested range, consistent with the FFT complexity; with only two distinct \(D\) (20k and 30k) no fit could discriminate exponents in \([2.3,2.7]\). Extrapolating \(D^{2.5}\) to \(D=50\,000\) projects \(6\)–\(10\) hours on the same hardware; this is an extrapolation, not a measurement.

Open problems

  1. Generalised filters. For primes \(\ell\equiv1\pmod3\), \(\ell\in\{13,19,31,\dots\}\), derive \(\mathcal F_\ell=\{(a\bmod\mathrm{ord}_\ell2,\,b\bmod\mathrm{ord}_\ell3):\ell\mid p\}\). For \(\ell=13\) the period is \(36\) and the filter removes a further \(\approx2/13\) of candidates; combining several small \(\ell\) may push pre-MR elimination above \(90\%\).

  2. Witnesses for general \(k\). For the broader family \(p=km(m+1)+\varepsilon\), identify the residue conditions under which a small fixed witness certifies the relevant factor, extending Theorems 10–14 beyond \(k=3\).

(The earlier open problem on the universality of \(w_2=5,w_3=7\) is now resolved, negatively, by Theorems 10–14; and the question of the cubic character of \(7\) for the full family \(p=3m^2+3m+1\) is resolved by Proposition 16.)

Conclusion

For the \(3\)-smooth subfamily \(p=3m(m+1)+1\), \(m=2^{a}3^{b}-1\), the Pocklington–Lehmer certificate is unconditional and minimal, and — the main new point — the two required witnesses are deterministically characterised: \(w_2=5\) is valid iff \(a-b\equiv1,2\pmod4\) (Theorem 10), and \(w_3=7\) iff \(m\not\equiv2\pmod7\) (Theorem 14), the latter via cubic reciprocity through the explicit Eisenstein factorisation \(p=(m+1)^3-m^3\). Together with three elementary filters removing \(\approx87\%\) of candidates, this yields a provably correct, low-overhead certification pipeline, demonstrated by certificates up to \(29\,998\) digits on commodity hardware. The contribution is structural and explicit rather than record-breaking: the value lies in identifying a family where both the certificate and its witnesses become exact arithmetic statements.

Verification by exact arithmetic

The witness theorems were checked against direct computation over all primes of the family with \(1\le a,b\le129\) (\(276\) primes; \(0\) exceptions).

from sympy import isprime
INVALID_w3 = {(0,1),(1,5),(2,3)}
vA = vB = n = 0
for a in range(1, 130):
    for b in range(1, 130):
        m = (1<<a)*pow(3,b) - 1
        p = 3*m*(m+1) + 1
        if not isprime(p): continue
        n += 1
        w2_valid = pow(5,(p-1)//2,p) != 1        # 5 is a quadratic non-residue
        w3_valid = pow(7,(p-1)//3,p) != 1        # 7 is a cubic non-residue
        if w2_valid != (((a-b) % 4) in (1,2)):           vA += 1   # Theorem 4.1
        predB = (m % 7 != 2) and ((a%3, b%6) not in INVALID_w3)
        if w3_valid != predB:                            vB += 1   # Theorem 4.5
print(n, vA, vB)        # -> 276 0 0

Proposition 16 (general cubic character of \(7\)) was checked over all \(p=3m^2+3m+1\) prime with \(m<9000\) (\(1797\) primes; \(0\) exceptions):

from sympy import isprime
RES21 = {0,2,10,18,20}                  # Proposition 4.7: residue classes mod 21
viol = n = 0
for m in range(2, 9000):
    p = 3*m*m + 3*m + 1
    if not isprime(p) or p == 7: continue
    n += 1
    if (pow(7,(p-1)//3,p)==1) != (m % 21 in RES21): viol += 1
print(n, viol)         # -> 1797 0

The \(29\,998\)-digit certificate is re-verified by four modular exponentiations:

a, b = 19435, 19173
m = 2**a * 3**b - 1;  p = 3*m*(m+1) + 1;  F = 2**a * 3**(b+1)
assert F*F > p
assert pow(5,p-1,p)==1 and __import__('math').gcd(pow(5,(p-1)//2,p)-1,p)==1
assert pow(7,p-1,p)==1 and __import__('math').gcd(pow(7,(p-1)//3,p)-1,p)==1
# => p is unconditionally prime
